\# Game Concept Document — Black Sky Bound



\## 1. Working Title



\*\*Black Sky Bound\*\*



\## 2. One-Sentence Pitch



A top-down action survival game about a young dragon fighting, fleeing, and surviving its way through a hostile world.



\## 3. Core Fantasy



The player gets to feel like:



A small but dangerous young dragon, newly alone in a chaotic world, surviving through instinct, speed, smoke, tooth, claw, and sudden bursts of terrifying power.



\## 4. Genre / Format



\* Genre: top-down 2D action survival

\* Camera/view: top-down 2D

\* Single-player / multiplayer: single-player

\* Session length: 10–20 minute levels

\* Target platform: PC first



\## 5. The Main Player Experience



In normal play, the player mostly:



\* Move quickly through dangerous spaces

\* Dodge enemies and environmental hazards

\* Attack with tooth, claw, body lunges, and smoke

\* Use positioning, terrain, and panic movement to survive

\* Escape the current scenario before being overwhelmed



\## 6. What Makes It Interesting



The game is not just another top-down action survival game because the player is the dangerous baby monster trying to survive everyone else’s war.



You are not clearing arenas like a hero; you are a small, frightened, vicious creature escaping a collapsing situation with tooth, claw, smoke, speed, and eventually fire.



\## 7. Core Loop



1\. Enter a dangerous area

2\. Read immediate threats and escape routes

3\. Dodge, attack, disrupt, or flee

4\. Survive long enough to reach safety

5\. Unlock the next scenario, ability, or story beat



\## 8. Emotional Tone



\### The game should feel:



\* feral

\* dangerous

\* chaotic

\* lonely

\* desperate

\* darkly beautiful



\### The game should not feel:



\* heroic

\* cosy

\* comedic

\* strategy-heavy

\* clean or arena-like

\* like a full power fantasy from the start



\## 9. World / Setting Snapshot



The game begins after the dragon’s nest is raided by men. Nearby, swarms of husks are attacking, the mother dragon is burning the forest in rage, and the young dragon is forced into the chaos alone.



\## 10. Player Role



The player is:



A young dragon separated from its nest.



The player wants:



To survive and escape the immediate danger.



The player is under pressure because:



The forest is collapsing into violence between raiders, husks, fire, and other disturbed predators.



\## 11. Key Systems — Only the Big Ones



\* Movement and dodging

\* Close-range attacks: tooth, claw, and body lunges

\* Smoke / disruption

\* Enemy pressure and escape



\## 12. Win / Loss / Progression



The player progresses by:



Surviving scenarios, reaching safety, and unlocking the next story beat or ability.



The player fails when:



The young dragon is killed, trapped, or overwhelmed before it can escape.



The player eventually unlocks or discovers:



Stronger survival instincts, new movement options, smoke control, and eventually dragonfire.



\## 13. Visual Direction



\### Reference words:



\* dark forest

\* ash and smoke

\* moonlit chaos

\* small creature, huge danger

\* violent silhouettes

\* glowing eyes and embers



\### Avoid:



\* cute mascot dragon

\* bright cartoon fantasy

\* clean arenas

\* readable-but-boring tiles

\* over-detailed realism

\* strategy-game visual clutter



\## 14. Scope Promise



For the first playable version, this game only needs:



A short top-down 2D survival action scenario where a young dragon moves through a dangerous forest, dodges threats, attacks at close range, uses smoke to disrupt enemies, and reaches safety before being overwhelmed.



The first playable does not need:



\* full stealth

\* full flight

\* base building

\* strategy systems

\* morale systems

\* complex fire simulation

\* open-world exploration

\* multiple playable characters

\* long-term survival crafting



\## 15. Unknowns / Risks



\### Things I still need to figure out:



\* How to keep the first playable small enough to finish without expanding back into a survival strategy game.

\* How to avoid falling into engine/tool-building work when the actual goal is shipping a playable game.

\* Whether tooth, claw, smoke, and movement feel satisfying before dragonfire is unlocked.

\* How much smoke/fire interaction is needed for fun without building a complex simulation too early.

\* How to make the young dragon feel vulnerable and dangerous at the same time.

\* What the shortest complete first scenario should be.
